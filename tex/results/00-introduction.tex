\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Write $\Prset$ for the set of primes.

\begin{conjecture}[Goldbach]\label{goldbach:conj}
Every even integer $n\geq4$ is a sum of two primes.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{definition}\label{pair:def}
A \dfnas{goldbach-pair}{Goldbach pair} for $n$ is an ordered pair $(p,q)$ of
primes with $p+q=n$.  An integer admitting at least one such pair is a
\dfnas{goldbach-number}{Goldbach number}, and the number of pairs it admits is
its \dfnas{goldbach-count}{Goldbach count} $\gc(n)$.
\end{definition}

In this language Conjecture~\ref{goldbach:conj} says that $\gc(n)\geq1$ for
every even $n\geq4$, and the natural quantitative strengthening --- that
$\gc(n)$ grows --- is the subject of the route in the companion edition.

This edition records three established statements.
Lemma~\ref{lower:lem} bounds a Goldbach number from below;
Lemma~\ref{reflect:lem} reduces the existence of a Goldbach pair to a set
intersection, which is what every computation here actually evaluates; and
Theorem~\ref{verify:thm} is the finite verification for $n\leq10^{8}$.
The initial range $n\leq12$ of that verification has additionally been checked
by machine, in Lean; see \texttt{formal/BLUEPRINT.md}.
